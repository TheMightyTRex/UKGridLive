Plug-in solar

On 27 August 2026, small "plug-in" (also called "balcony") solar panel kits became legal to sell and self-install in Great Britain for the first time - a product category already common in Germany, now permitted here under new government rules. This page explains what's actually allowed, and includes a calculator estimating what one might save you, using Ofgem's published price cap rather than any supplier's own marketing numbers.

What is it?

A plug-in solar kit is one or more small solar panels wired to a "microinverter" that plugs directly into an ordinary wall socket using a standard UK plug - no rewiring, no consumer unit work, and (for a suitable existing installation) no electrician required. While the panels are generating, the power feeds straight into whichever appliances in your home are switched on at that moment - a kettle, a fridge, a TV - reducing how much you import from the grid. There's no export payment mechanism for these small unmetered systems: electricity you generate but don't use at that instant is simply lost, which is why how much time you spend at home during daylight hours matters as much as panel size for how much you'll actually save.

This is a distinct, much smaller-scale product from a full rooftop solar PV installation (typically 3-5kW, professionally installed, MCS-certified, often paired with a battery and an export tariff). Plug-in kits are capped at a fraction of that size by design - see the rules below - and are aimed at renters, leaseholders and anyone who wants a small amount of self-generated power without a full installation.

The rules

The government's regulations (laid in Parliament on 17 July 2026, in force from 27 August 2026) and the accompanying Interim Product Specification set firm technical limits - a compliant kit isn't just "any solar panel you can plug in":

  • Maximum inverter output800VA / 3.5A AC - roughly 800W into your home's circuit, whatever the panels' own rating
  • Maximum panel capacity2,000W DC across up to 4 panels (max. 2 in series per string) - oversizing panels beyond the 800W inverter cap is normal and improves low-light output, at the cost of some "clipping" on bright days
  • Connection methodDirectly into a standard socket via the kit's own manufacturer-supplied BS 1363 plug - extension leads, multi-way adaptors, converters and RCD adaptors are all prohibited
  • Plug fuse ratingNon-rewireable plug, fused at no more than 5A (BS 1362)
  • RegistrationRequired via the national myplugin.solar portal, which notifies your electricity network operator on your behalf (the simplified route to the existing G98 network-notification requirement)
  • Devices per householdOne, under the current G98 limit - due to relax to one per individual circuit once G98 itself is formally amended
  • Self-installPermitted for a suitable existing installation; professional assessment is recommended for older fuse boards, unidentified circuits, damaged sockets, or panel arrays above 960W

A handful of things can still stop you fitting one even though the product itself is now legal: renters and leaseholders need their landlord's or freeholder's permission; some buildings (those with certain cladding, or undergoing remediation work) are excluded outright; mounting to bare timber isn't permitted, and cable ties or tape alone aren't an acceptable fixing; and England's permitted development rules limit how far a panel can project from a wall (40cm generally, 20cm near a highway) or a pitched roof (20cm), with a 60cm height limit on a flat roof. Scotland and Wales have their own planning regimes. As of this page's last check, the ENA's own Type Test Register listed a mix of compliant and non-compliant devices submitted by manufacturers - always check a specific kit against the live register before buying, rather than trusting a retailer's own compliance claim.

It does not model shading, an export tariff, a battery, or your own actual tariff, which may not be the price cap.

What kits actually cost

Prices vary a lot depending on whether you self-install or pay for a professional, MCS-certified fit:

  • Self-install kit, ~800W panelsRoughly £300-£600
  • Professionally installed, single ~400W panelFrom around £800
  • Professionally installed, twin ~800W panelsFrom around £1,400

These are indicative ranges from retailer/installer pricing pages, not this site's own quotes, and will vary by retailer and kit. See Sources below.
