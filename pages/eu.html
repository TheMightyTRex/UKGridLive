EU countries: a combined snapshot of the member states this site tracks

The EU's 27 member states run separate national grids, tightly interconnected and increasingly traded as one synchronous market. This page isn't a full EU-27 total - it sums live figures across the member states this site tracks via the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform (Ireland, France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Denmark, Germany, Spain, Italy, Sweden and Portugal), which still leaves out other EU grids including Poland, Austria and the Baltic and Balkan states. Treat it as a live snapshot of ten EU grids, not a bloc-wide figure.

      About the EU-27 aggregate

      Continental Europe's grid - from Portugal to Poland, and as far as the Balkans - runs as one large synchronous area coordinated by ENTSO-E, the European Network of Transmission System Operators for Electricity. Within it, national mixes differ hugely: France generates roughly two-thirds of its power from nuclear, Poland still leans heavily on coal, Norway and Austria are dominated by hydro, and Denmark regularly covers all of its demand with wind alone on windy days.

      Cross-border interconnection lets surplus power flow to where it's needed: German wind exported south on a windy afternoon, French nuclear exported north on a still winter evening. The EU's target of a fully integrated internal electricity market - and its REPowerEU and Fit for 55 programmes - are steadily pushing member states toward more shared infrastructure and faster renewable buildout.

      Great Britain, having left the EU's internal energy market after Brexit, now trades with the bloc only through bilateral interconnectors (to France, Belgium and the Netherlands) rather than as part of the coupled EU market - which is why GB's flows appear here as "net non-EU trade".

      About the wider EU-27

      The ten grids tracked above cover a good share of the EU-27's population and generation capacity, including its two largest economies (Germany and France) and several of its most renewable-heavy grids (Sweden, Portugal, Denmark), but still leave out Poland, Austria, the Baltic states, the Balkans and others, so nothing on this page should be read as a genuine EU-wide total or record. For actual EU-27-wide figures and records, the Eurostat energy statistics and the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform (covering all connected TSOs, not just the ten this site tracks) are the standard references.

      Data sources for this page

      The Elexon Insights Solution, NESO Data Portal and Carbon Intensity API that power the Great Britain pages are GB-specific and don't cover the EU. This page instead sums the same live per-country ENTSO-E Transparency Platform feed that powers Ireland, France, Netherlands, Belgium, Denmark, Germany, Spain, Italy, Sweden and Portugal's own pages - see each of those pages, or the sitewide data sources page, for details on that feed and its limitations. Norway (NO2) is also tracked via the same feed but excluded from this page's totals, since Norway isn't an EU member state.

      A genuine EU-27 aggregate would need every connected TSO's data, not just these ten - via the full ENTSO-E Transparency Platform and Eurostat's energy statistics. That's a substantially larger integration than this site currently does, so it isn't attempted here.
