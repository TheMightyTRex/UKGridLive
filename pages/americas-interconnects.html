How the grids of the Americas connect to each other

Unlike Great Britain and its subsea interconnectors to continental Europe, none of the grids covered on this site's Americas pages are electrically connected to GB at all - the Atlantic has no HVDC cable crossing it for power. This page instead covers how the grids within the Americas connect to one another: which countries share synchronous grids, which are tied together by HVDC links, and the handful of jointly-operated hydro dams that straddle national borders.

North America: three asynchronous interconnections

The USA, Canada and northern Mexico share three large synchronous interconnections rather than one single grid - within each, every generator runs locked to the same AC frequency; between them, power can only cross via HVDC back-to-back ties that convert to direct current and back, rather than flowing directly.

  • Eastern Interconnection - covers most of the US east of the Rockies, plus Ontario, Quebec (partially), Manitoba and the Maritime provinces of Canada. IESO's Ontario grid, covered on this site's Canada page, sits inside this interconnection.
  • Western Interconnection - covers the western US, British Columbia, Alberta and a sliver of northern Mexico (Baja California).
  • ERCOT (Texas) - covers most of Texas, deliberately kept separate from the other two (and largely outside US federal FERC jurisdiction) specifically to avoid interstate power flows.

Quebec's grid (Hydro-Québec) is a partial exception: it's nominally part of the Eastern Interconnection's synchronous area geographically, but is operated asynchronously, tied to its neighbours (Ontario, the US Northeast, the Maritimes) only via HVDC links rather than direct AC connection - largely a historical/operational choice by Hydro-Québec rather than a geographic necessity.

Mexico's main grid, the Sistema Eléctrico Nacional operated by CENACE, connects to the US Western and Eastern Interconnections via several small HVDC and AC ties near the border, and is not otherwise synchronised with either.

South America: shared dams and cross-border hydro

South America's grids are less uniformly interconnected than Europe's - most trade with immediate neighbours only, often built around specific shared infrastructure rather than a continent-wide synchronous grid:

  • Itaipu Dam (Brazil-Paraguay) - one of the largest hydroelectric plants ever built, jointly owned and operated by Brazil and Paraguay on the border river. Paraguay uses only a fraction of its half of the output domestically and exports the rest to Brazil, making Itaipu a major, permanent cross-border power flow rather than an occasional trade.
  • Yacyretá Dam (Argentina-Paraguay) - a second large jointly-operated hydro dam, on the Paraná river between the two countries, feeding both grids.
  • Argentina-Brazil-Uruguay - connected via a mix of AC and HVDC back-to-back ties, allowing significant trade, particularly of Argentine and Brazilian surplus into Uruguay's smaller, wind-and-hydro-heavy grid.
  • Chile-Argentina - several cross-border ties exist, historically used more for emergency backup and limited exchange than large sustained commercial flows, reflecting Chile's SEN being a long, narrow, largely self-contained system.
  • Colombia-Ecuador and Colombia-Venezuela - Colombia trades with both neighbours, though the Venezuela link has seen intermittent operation in recent years tied to that country's own grid reliability issues.

Unlike ENTSO-E's single pan-European transparency platform, there's no equivalent single data source covering cross-border flows for all of South America - each pair of interconnected countries' actual traded volumes would need querying from each country's own operator individually, which this site doesn't currently do (see each country's own Data sources section for what is and isn't wired up).

None of this connects to Great Britain

To be explicit: nothing on this page, or on any of this site's Americas pages, has any electrical connection whatsoever to Great Britain's own grid. GB's actual interconnectors are all with continental Europe and Ireland - see the GB Interconnectors page. The USA, Canada, Brazil, Argentina, Chile and Colombia are included on this site purely so their figures can be compared against GB's and against each other on the Comparisons page, not because of any physical grid relationship.
